# No obligation vs Must disclose: what 5 frontier AI models actually said

**The question:** A consultant delivers a client report that they directed and edited but a model drafted. Are they obligated to disclose that a model wrote the first draft?

We asked 5 frontier models this exact question, at the same time, with the same instructions, and made each one commit to a single answer instead of hedging. Here is how they split.

## The scoreboard

| Pick | Models | Count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **No obligation** | Claude Opus, GPT-5.6 Sol, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Llama 4 Maverick | 4 |
| **Must disclose** | DeepSeek V3.1 | 1 |

## Every answer, unedited

### Claude Opus <span>Anthropic</span>

**Picked: No obligation**

> Clients contract for the consultant's judgment, direction, and accountability over the final work product, not for a specific drafting method, so the tool used to produce an early draft is generally immaterial. Disclosure becomes necessary only if there's a contractual term, confidentiality concern, or explicit representation that no such tools were used.

### GPT-5.6 Sol <span>OpenAI</span>

**Picked: No obligation**

> If the consultant directed, verified, and accepts responsibility for the report, using a model for the first draft does not inherently require disclosure. Disclosure is required only if a contract, law, professional rule, or client policy mandates it.

### Gemini 3.1 Pro <span>Google</span>

**Picked: No obligation**

> Consultants are hired for their expertise and take full professional responsibility for the final deliverable, regardless of the tools used to draft it. Unless explicitly required by the client contract, there is no general ethical or legal duty to disclose the use of AI as a drafting assistant.

### Llama 4 Maverick <span>Meta</span>

**Picked: No obligation**

> The consultant directed and edited the report, taking ownership of its content, and the use of a model to draft it is a tool used in the process, not necessarily relevant to the client. Disclosure of the drafting process is not inherently required for the report's validity or the consultant's responsibility.

### DeepSeek V3.1 <span>DeepSeek</span>

**Picked: Must disclose**

> Transparency is required to maintain trust and avoid misleading the client about the origin of the work. Failing to disclose could be seen as misrepresenting the consultant's direct authorship.

## The verdict

All three answers effectively convey that there is no obligation to disclose the use of a model for the first draft, making them equally valid in addressing the user's question. A meaningful difference is that Claude Opus emphasizes the immateriality of the drafting method, while GPT-5.6 Sol and Gemini 3.1 Pro focus more on the consultant's responsibility and the lack of general ethical or legal duty to disclose.

## Why this is worth two minutes

Neither answer here is wrong. That is exactly the problem with asking one model: you get a confident, well-written answer with no indication that another equally capable model would have told you the opposite. The disagreement is information, and a single-model workflow throws it away by construction.
